## What it asks

Each party generates its own key independently and posts it. The joint encryption
key is a deterministic public function of the posted keys. No distributed key
generation protocol, no interaction, only a PKI.

## Why it matters for encrypted mempools

Wagner's own criterion is conditional: *if the number of parties is large, avoid
DKG*. A DKG is a liveness hazard and must be re-run whenever the committee
rotates. If the committee is a small rotating subset of a large validator pool,
the per-rotation DKG cost dominates. For a fixed, small keyper set, a one-time DKG
is simpler and adequate, so this desideratum is deployment-dependent rather than
absolute.

## State of the art

Classically solved: [[gkpw-silent-setup]] from pairings, and
[[hall-andersen-simkin-wagner-silent]] generically from key-anonymous PKE.
Post-quantum, this is the frontier: [[champion-wu-dbe]] and
[[champion-wu-monotone-dnf]] give the lattice route, but as constructions and
proofs rather than deployed systems.

## Combining it with batching

Silent setup and batched decryption were achieved independently for years.
[[beast-mev]] was the first to get both at once, over pairings. Doing both
post-quantum is still open; see [[silent-pq-threshold-t-of-n]].
