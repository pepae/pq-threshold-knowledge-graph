## What it asks

Decryption must be computationally cheap. Wagner's parenthetical is the whole
point: *this is on the critical path*.

## Why it matters for encrypted mempools

A block cannot be executed until its transactions are decrypted. Latency here is
latency in block production, so it competes with the slot time rather than with
a user's patience.

## Where schemes stand

Pairing-based batched schemes are in the seconds for an Ethereum-sized block:
about 2.8 s per committee member for roughly 500 transactions in [[cgpp-bte]],
about 3.2 s in [[choudhuri-garg-policharla-wang-onetime]], under 440 ms for 512
transactions in [[beat-mev]]. The PQ instantiation is not in the same regime:
50 s partial decryption and 327 s to combine at batch 512, per
[[beatmev-pq-implementation]]. That measurement, more than any asymptotic
statement, is what a post-quantum encrypted mempool has to fix.

## Reading the measurement carefully

Distinguish what is on the critical path from what is not. Proof verification and
well-formedness checks can often be done off the critical path when a ciphertext
first arrives, rather than at decryption time.
