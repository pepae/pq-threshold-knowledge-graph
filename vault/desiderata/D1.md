## What it asks

No party may hold a secret that, if retained, breaks the scheme. A structured
reference string generated by a ceremony is acceptable only if the toxic waste is
provably discardable; a setup whose trapdoor *is* the secret key is not.

## Why it matters for encrypted mempools

A mempool committee is adversarial by assumption. Any residual trapdoor is a
permanent front-running backdoor, and one that leaves no trace when used.

## How schemes fail it

Pairing-based silent-setup schemes typically need powers-of-tau. [[gkpw-silent-setup]]
needs a trusted setup on top of its silent key aggregation. The BTX line takes
"powers of tau with a hole" where tau is the secret key, which is the sharpest
version of the problem. Lattice schemes are usually better placed here: a public
matrix expanded from a seed is transparent by construction.

## Distinguish from D2

D1 is about *trapdoors*. [[D2]] is about *interaction*. A scheme can have a
transparent setup and still need a DKG, or a silent setup and still need a
ceremony.
