## What it asks

A decryption share is bound to a context, typically the block or batch it was
produced for. Shares generated under different contexts must not combine. If
fewer than t shares exist under context A and fewer than t under context B, the
union must still not decrypt, even when it exceeds t in total.

## Why it matters for encrypted mempools

This is the anti-harvesting property, and it is specifically a *consensus*
requirement rather than a cryptographic nicety. Competing forks are different
contexts. Without context binding, a committee that partially signs on two forks
leaks a decryption it never authorised on either.
